Yaphank + Richford · Closed August 2026

Two closings, two MLS boards, one client

My client was selling his longtime home in Yaphank and buying upstate in Richford, more than 200 miles away. The purchase could only happen if the sale closed. I knew the Yaphank house well: I had leased its apartment twice, over asking both times. It first went on the market in late 2025 at $624,999 and came off. In spring 2026 I relisted it at $650,000, with the carpet pulled up, the floors refinished and the whole place power-washed and cleaned out by my crew. It was in contract at $700,000 in 12 days.

Then came the hard part. Getting from contract to closing took four months, several extended deadlines and daily follow-up, all while keeping the upstate purchase alive with a different MLS, different attorneys and different closing customs. Both deals closed in August 2026, days apart. He got $50,000 over asking and the upstate home he wanted.

Why these numbers are different

I pulled every closed single-family sale on Long Island over two years and compared homes whose listings described a specific update against similar homes in the same town and quarter whose listings described none. Same size, lot, beds, baths, age, style, basement, garage and waterfront. What's left is the price difference buyers paid for the update itself. Two things stood out: a fixer sells for roughly a fifth less than the same home fully updated, and updates don't simply stack — a home with a new kitchen usually has other work done too, so combined projects are counted with a discount, and a full renovation always comes out on top.

Then I price the work as the builder. That's the part no other agent can give you: what it's worth and what it costs, from one person.
